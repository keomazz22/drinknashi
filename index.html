The Land

Where the Alps
meet the Padana Plain

Centallo sits at the convergence of two worlds: the cold, crystalline air descending from the Maritime Alps and the warmth of the Padana plain below. This collision creates a microclimate of extraordinary complexity — the precise conditions that the Nashi pear demands to develop its signature crunch and sweetness.

The Nashi pear requires a minimum of 600–900 chilling hours per winter — temperatures below 7°C sustained long enough to force dormancy, then a warm, long growing season to develop flavour. Centallo provides exactly that. No other location in Piedmont has been found to match it.

Over 37 hectares of meticulously managed orchard — divided between the home estate of 20 hectares and a second plot of 17 hectares — the Trybeca trees are pruned, thinned, and irrigated by hand, following the philosophy that has guided the Oderda family for three generations.
