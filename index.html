The Terroir

From the Plains
of Piedmont

Nestled between the Maritime Alps and the Po Valley, Centallo enjoys a unique microclimate — warm summers tempered by cool Alpine breezes that slow the fruit's maturation, developing extraordinary complexity of flavour within each Nashi pear.

Trybeca's orchards are tended with the same reverence afforded to the region's celebrated wines. The Nashi tree demands patience — it takes years before bearing fruit worthy of the Trybeca standard. Those that do not meet it are returned to the land. Only perfection is bottled.

The result is a juice that carries within it the identity of a place: the mineral richness of alpine soil, the luminous Italian sun, the clear cold waters of the Piedmontese plain. This is not simply juice — it is a geography in a bottle.

From Orchard to Bottle

The Art of Zero Compromise

Each stage of production is performed with the same obsessive attention to quality that defines everything Trybeca does.

I
Cultivation

Patient Growing

Nashi pear trees are cultivated in Trybeca's own orchards in Centallo, under strict organic protocols. The trees are pruned by hand, fed naturally, and monitored throughout the growing season to ensure optimal fruit development.

II
Harvest

Hand Selection

At the precise moment of peak ripeness — determined by sugar content, texture, and aroma — each pear is harvested by hand. Any fruit that does not meet the standard is excluded. No exceptions, no compromise.

III
Processing

Cold Pressing

The pears are cold-pressed within hours of harvest to preserve their full nutritional profile, natural enzymes, and delicate aromatics. A minimal amount of lemon is added — not to flavour, but to preserve the juice's luminous golden colour naturally.

IV
Bottling

Sealed in Glass

Bottled by La Manta Food srl in premium 250ml glass — the only vessel worthy of the contents. No plastic, no BPA, no compromise on the integrity of the product. Each bottle is then numbered and prepared for its journey to you.

ODERDA
— The People Behind the Pear —

The Oderda Family
& the Art of Cultivation

For over sixty years, one Piedmontese family has been quietly rewriting the rules of Italian fruit farming. In Centallo — a town of 6,000 souls in the Po Valley, cradled between the Maritime Alps and the flatlands of Cuneo — the Oderda family has built a living legacy of innovation, patience, and devotion to the land.

G
Giovanni Oderda
Founder & Visionary

Giovanni Oderda is the father of Trybeca in every sense of the word — founder, farmer, and dreamer. A man described by those who know him as perpetually ahead of his time, he coined the very name Trybeca as a fusion of three English verbs — try, be, can — a personal manifesto before the word existed. In the 1960s he was one of the first in the Cuneo province to cultivate nectarines. In 1972 he pioneered kiwi production in Piedmont. In 1986, when the Nashi pear was still an exotic curiosity in Europe, he planted the first orchard — buying the inaugural plants directly from France with a conviction that this extraordinary fruit belonged in Italian soil.

F
Fabrizio Oderda
Co-Owner & Head of Production

Fabrizio grew up with soil under his fingernails and Nashi pear trees as his classroom. Today he oversees the entire production chain of Trybeca's 20 hectares — monitoring the trees through every season, managing the harvest windows, and ensuring that every pear that leaves the orchard is worthy of the Trybeca name. It was Fabrizio who recalled that their journey with Nashi began in 1988 with those first French plants, and it is his hands-on obsession with quality that has made Trybeca the undisputed reference point for Nashi made in Italy.

S
Sabrina Oderda
Co-Owner & Commercial Direction

Sabrina is the voice of Trybeca to the world. Fabrizio's sister brings the same depth of passion to the commercial and communicative side of the business that her brother brings to the fields. Under her direction, Trybeca's Nashi has grown from a regional curiosity into a product recognised at a national level — appearing on Italian television's La7, featured in agricultural and food media, and now evolving into Nashi juice: the next chapter of the Rivoluzione Verde.

GM
Gian Maria Oderda
Next Generation · Age 25

The youngest face of Trybeca, Gian Maria is Fabrizio's son and the third generation of the Oderda family to grow up among the Nashi orchards of Centallo. At 25, he already carries the weight of a legacy that spans over sixty years — and the energy of someone who knows exactly what it means to inherit an orchard that took decades to build. Gian Maria represents the future of Trybeca: a new generation that has absorbed the family's obsession with quality since childhood, and is now ready to carry the Rivoluzione Verde into the next chapter.

Six Decades of
Green Revolution

1960s
Pioneers of the Nectarine

Giovanni Oderda becomes one of the first farmers in the Cuneo province to cultivate nectarines commercially, establishing Trybeca's identity as a farm that leads, not follows.

1972
The Kiwi Gamble

At a time when kiwi was virtually unknown in Piedmont, Giovanni plants his first kiwi vines — over 40 years before the fruit becomes mainstream. Once again, the Oderda farm is ahead of history.

1986
The Nashi Arrives in Italy

The first Nashi pear orchards are planted at Centallo, purchased from French nurseries. Most other producers who experiment with the Asian pear eventually give up. The Oderda family does not. They double down.

2000s
Monoculture Mastery

Trybeca converts entirely to Nashi as its singular focus. The orchards expand to 20 hectares of owned land, plus an additional 17 hectares of affiliated farms throughout the Centallo area — creating the largest dedicated Nashi operation in Italy.

2021
The Juice is Born

Trybeca launches its Nashi juice — il nashi da bere — taking their finest pears and pressing them into the bottle you now hold. 100% fruit. No compromise. The Rivoluzione Verde continues.

The Growing Calendar

Why the Nashi Cannot Be Rushed

The Asian pear is one of the most demanding fruits in cultivation. It requires specific cold temperatures in winter, precise timing in spring, relentless attention in summer, and a harvest window measured in days — not weeks. This is why Nashi cannot be farmed everywhere, and why only certain months produce fruit worthy of the Trybeca standard.

❄️ Winter
December — February
Dormancy & Chilling

The Nashi tree requires between 600 and 900 hours of cold temperatures below 7°C to properly enter dormancy and reset for the following year. The Alpine winters of Centallo — cold, clean, and consistent — provide exactly this. Without sufficient chilling hours, bud break is erratic, flowering is poor, and the harvest fails. Piedmont's winters are not a hardship for the Nashi tree. They are a necessity. The Oderda family monitors temperature logs across the orchards throughout winter, ensuring every tree receives what it needs.

🌸 Spring
March — May
Blossom & Thinning

In March, the orchards burst into white blossom — thousands of delicate flowers covering the Nashi trees in a display that lasts only days. Cross-pollination is critical: the Oderda family staggers their three varieties — Try, Plumpy, and Deli — to ensure the blooming windows overlap perfectly. Then begins one of the most labour-intensive tasks in fruit farming: hand-thinning. Every excess fruit cluster must be removed by hand so that the tree concentrates its energy into fewer, superior pears. No machine can do this with the precision it demands.

☀️ Summer
June — August
Development & Care

Throughout the long Piedmontese summer, the Nashi pears develop slowly under the Italian sun, their skins transitioning from green to their characteristic golden-bronze. The Oderda team walks the orchards daily — monitoring irrigation from the Stura di Demonte river, adjusting nutrient cycles, and protecting the fruit from the summer storms that roll in from the Alps. The pear's famously crisp texture and high natural sugar content develop during these months of patient, attentive care.

🍐 Autumn
August — October
Harvest

Unlike most fruits, the Nashi pear reaches its peak quality only when fully ripened on the tree. The harvest window for each variety lasts just days — the difference between a good pear and an exceptional one is measured in hours. Fabrizio personally determines the precise day of harvest for each orchard block. The three Trybeca varieties are staggered across late August through October, extending the season and ensuring that each pear is pressed at its absolute pinnacle.

The Three Varieties

Trybeca cultivates three distinct Nashi varieties — each with its own character, origin, and harvest window — together forming the complex, layered flavour profile of every bottle of Nashi juice.

Try
Japanese Origin

Named with characteristic Trybeca spirit — a direct nod to the family's "try, be, can" philosophy — the Try variety is of Japanese descent, bringing the floral delicacy and refined sweetness that made the Nashi pear legendary in its homeland. Harvests in late August to early September.

Plumpy
Korean Origin

Rounded, generous, and intensely juicy, the Plumpy variety brings Korean Nashi at its most expressive. Its fruit is larger than the Try, with a bolder sweetness and a slightly longer window to peak ripeness — typically mid-September to early October.

The October Drop
Deli
Korean Origin

The Deli is the one. Ripening last — deep into October, when the Alpine air of Centallo grows sharp and cold — it is the variety that gives Nashi juice its soul. Its skin turns the deepest golden-bronze of the three, and its juice is the most concentrated, mineral, and complex of all. Where Try brings elegance and Plumpy brings body, Deli brings depth — a lingering finish that recalls the earth, the cold air, and sixty years of family devotion. Every bottle of this first Nashi drop is pressed from the Deli harvest. This is what October tastes like in Centallo.
